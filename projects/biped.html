Planar Bipedal Robot

A five-link, four-actuator biped on a boom-constrained walking rig, built for my MSc dissertation at Bournemouth University and continued as grant-funded research. 3D-printed mechanics, ODrive actuation over CAN, a ROS 2 stack written from scratch, and gaits from trajectory optimisation on the full dynamic model.

OVERVIEW

The robot walks in a vertical plane, held by a boom that pivots around a central mast. It extends a single-leg design from Prof. Alexander Badri-Spröwitz's group (Mo et al., Scientific Reports, 2023) into a complete robot, with eleven documented design changes. Its architecture matches RABBIT, the point-foot, underactuated biped on which Hybrid Zero Dynamics was developed.

Each leg has two actuated joints: hip (±90°) and knee (−8.9° to +43.3°). A passive parallelogram linkage keeps the foot parallel to the ground through the full knee range, with no extra actuator.

MECHANICAL DESIGN

The structure is 3D printed; the drive parts are steel, bought in and modified in-house after metal printing and EDM proved impractical. The knee motor sits near the hip to keep leg inertia low for fast swing.

  • Total leg mass (both legs): ~2.7 kg
  • Thigh 149.5 mm  ·  shank 150 mm
  • Gear ratios: hip 5.000 : 1  ·  knee 8.333 : 1

ELECTRONICS, ACTUATION & SENSING

Four T-Motor MN7005 motors, each on its own ODrive S1, share one 1 Mbps CAN bus. CUI AMT21 RS-485 absolute encoders keep joint position across power cycles, and load cells (HX711) measure force. ROS 2 Jazzy on a Linux PC commands and logs all four joints in sync.

THE ODRIVE GEAR-RATIO BUG

The vendored odrive_ros2_control driver declares a gear_ratio parameter but never applies it, so each joint moved only 1/gear_ratio of the command. Rather than patch the C++ driver, gear_ratios.py converts joint ↔ motor angles for every hardware node, and joint_state_corrector.py republishes corrected joint states for RViz. The knee ratio itself was found empirically: 7.353 → 8.667 → 8.333.

SOFTWARE & SIMULATION

The ROS 2 workspace was built from scratch: robot description (URDF/SDF, launch, ros2_control config), control (IK, calibration, gear ratios), a Bézier foot-trajectory planner with its own designer GUI, a gait coordinator, and the vendored ODrive driver. One ros2_control interface is shared by Gazebo and the hardware.

Simulation runs on Gazebo Harmonic with Bullet physics, chosen because the default DART engine rejects closed kinematic loops. A hand-authored SDF closes the four-bar loop physically through par_lower_joint.

CONTROL

With the parallelogram constraint (θankle = −θknee), each leg reduces to a closed-form 2R problem:

cos(φ2) = (py² + pz² − L1² − L2²) / (2·L1·L2)
θhip = atan2(py, −pz) − atan2(L2sinφ2, L1+L2cosφ2)

Early gaits were Bézier foot trajectories, sampled at 250 Hz and solved through IK, with Pinocchio (RNEA) computing the inverse-dynamics torques along them.

HARDWARE FINDING

Compliant position gains gave about 0.6 Hz of tracking bandwidth, against the 5–10 Hz a swing phase needs. That gap made the case for model-based torque feedforward.

Trajectory-optimised gaits. Gaits now come from a constrained optimal-control problem on the full dynamic model (soft touchdown, smooth weight transfer, friction and motor limits), minimising energy and landing impact. It is transcribed in CasADi, solved with IPOPT, and outputs feedforward torques for the joint loops. The model is verified against Pinocchio, the gaits validated in simulation, and first trials run on the robot.

STATUS & NEXT STEPS

Walking trials isolated five specific mechanical faults, each with a planned fix; foot slip is now the limiting factor. Next:

  • Fix the five mechanical faults found in the trials
  • Solve foot slip, the current limit on optimised walking
  • Hardware paper for the Journal of Open Hardware (in preparation), then papers on the ROS 2 architecture and trajectory-optimised gaits
